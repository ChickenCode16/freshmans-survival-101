Use one folder for originals and one for photocopies.

Check requirements before traveling to campus.

Photograph receipts and confirmation slips.

Ask for written instructions if there are many steps.

Syllabus

Use your syllabus to plan ahead and understand what each course expects.

Core Information

What Is a Syllabus?

A syllabus is a course guide explaining what you will study, what work you will do, and how the class is organized.

Why It Matters

It helps you check deadlines, rules, and requirements instead of relying on memory.

How to Read One

Start with course expectations and grading. Then check the schedule and policies and write important dates down.

Course Description

Introduces the subject and the main skills you are expected to gain.

Topics and Schedule

Lists lessons and activities. The order may change, so listen for updates.

Grading System

Explains the types of work used to calculate your grade and percentage.

Quizzes and Projects

Lists assignments, projects, quizzes, and how to submit them.

Class Participation

Explains whether discussions, attendance, or activities are part of the course.

Attendance and Late Work

Check each instructor's rules in terms of class attendance.

Tips and Tricks

Read the entire syllabus during the first week.

Add major deadlines to your calendar immediately.

Keep a copy where you can find it quickly.

Ask early if a requirement is unclear.

Common Mistakes

Never Reading It

You may miss grading, attendance, or assignment rules.

Missing Deadlines

Write dates down instead of expecting to remember them.

Assuming Every Course Is the Same

Check each instructor's expectations separately.

GPA Scale

4.00 — Highest
3.00 — Above average
2.00 — Average
1.00 — Lowest

Scale meanings may vary by school; check your syllabus for the official grading system.
